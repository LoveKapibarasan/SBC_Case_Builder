# Vented Split Panel

A hex vented cover panel of any size, automatically split into tiles that fit the
print bed and locked together with **printed butterfly keys**. No bought hardware.

Defaults to the footprint of [the SSI-EEB frame](./sbccb_eeb_frame.md),
309.8 x 335.2 mm, which splits into a 2 x 2 grid of 154.9 x 167.6 mm tiles.

Generated by `sbccb_eeb_panel.scad`.

![panel](./assets/sbccb_eeb_panel_iso.png)

## Why butterfly keys

A 309.8 x 335.2 panel needs at least a 2 x 2 tile grid on a 220 mm bed. A sliding
joint such as a dovetail cannot assemble a 2 x 2 grid, because the last tile would
have to slide in two directions at once.

Butterfly keys drop in **vertically**, so tile order does not matter. Each seam is
a plain butt joint; a bowtie shaped key sits in a pocket straddling the seam and
locks the two tiles in tension. The keys also align the tiles during assembly,
so no separate dowels are needed.

![seam key](./assets/sbccb_eeb_panel_seam.png)

## Structure

Each tile is a thin vented field with a solid rib around its perimeter:

- **field** `panel_t` 3 mm thick, hex cells 8 mm across the flats, 2.5 mm walls
- **rib** `rib_w` 14 mm wide and `rib_t` 6 mm thick

Ribs double up along every seam, giving a 28 mm solid band. That band is what
hosts the key pockets and what makes the finished panel stiff. Only whole hex
cells are generated, so every tile edge is clean and full strength.

Everything prints flat side down, no support.

All four tiles have the same volume because in a 2 x 2 grid every tile has exactly
two interior edges. They are still four different parts, the pockets are on
different sides.

A tile is larger than half the bed, so **print one tile per batch, four batches
total**. The twelve keys fit alongside any one of them.

## Airflow and material

| | |
| --- | --- |
| open area inside the vent field | 58.0 % |
| vent field as a share of the panel | 68.2 % |
| **open area over the whole panel** | **39.6 %** |
| total open area | 411 cm2 |
| solid volume | 302 cm3 |
| PLA, printed near solid | roughly 350 g |

A hex field this thin prints as almost pure perimeter, so treat it as close to
100 % dense rather than applying an infill fraction.

350 g is about a third of a spool. That is still well under the price of a bought
mesh panel, but it is a long print. Lighter presets:

| preset | `panel_t` | `cell_size` | `cell_wall` | open area | PLA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| default | 3 | 8 | 2.5 | 39.6 % | ~356 g |
| light | 2 | 10 | 2 | 47.4 % | ~299 g |
| lighter | 2 | 12 | 2 | 50.1 % | ~292 g |
| stiff | 4 | 8 | 3 | 36.1 % | ~411 g |

The ribs are about a third of the total volume. Dropping `rib_t` to 5 or `rib_w`
to 12 saves material at some cost in stiffness.

## Printing

```sh
OSC="/c/Program Files/OpenSCAD/openscad.com"

for c in 0 1; do for r in 0 1; do
  "$OSC" -o "stl/eeb_panel/tile_${c}_${r}.stl" \
    -D 'view="part"' -D 'part="tile"' -D "tile_col=$c" -D "tile_row=$r" \
    sbccb_eeb_panel.scad
done; done

"$OSC" -o "stl/eeb_panel/seam_key.stl" -D 'view="part"' -D 'part="key"' \
  sbccb_eeb_panel.scad
```

Suggested settings: 0.2 mm layers, 4 perimeters, PLA or PETG. Infill barely
matters, the field is all perimeter anyway.

### Key fit

`key_fit` is the clearance per face between key and pocket, 0.1 mm by default,
so a key is 0.2 mm smaller than its pocket across every dimension. Print one key
and one tile corner first and check the fit before committing to four tiles:

- key falls in loosely, raise `key_fit` toward 0 or go slightly negative
- key will not seat, raise `key_fit` to 0.15 or 0.2

Keys are meant to be tapped in with light pressure and stay put.

## Parameters

| parameter | default | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `panel_x` / `panel_y` | 309.8 / 335.2 | overall panel size, matches the frame outline |
| `panel_t` | 3 | vented field thickness |
| `rib_w` / `rib_t` | 14 / 6 | perimeter rib, `rib_w` must exceed `key_len`/2 |
| `cell_size` / `cell_wall` | 8 / 2.5 | hex across the flats, and material between cells |
| `bed_x` / `bed_y` | 220 / 220 | drives the tile count automatically |
| `bed_margin` | 8 | kept clear at the bed edges when choosing the tile count |
| `key_len` / `key_end` / `key_waist` | 24 / 12 / 7 | butterfly key shape |
| `key_depth` | 4 | pocket depth in the 6 mm rib |
| `key_fit` | 0.1 | clearance per face |
| `keys_per_seam` | 3 | keys per tile edge |

The tile grid is derived, not set: `cols = ceil(panel_x / (bed_x - bed_margin))`.
Change `panel_x`, `panel_y` or the bed size and the tiling re-solves itself.

## Not done yet

- **Attachment to the frame.** The panel currently stands alone. Options are
  printed snap hooks on the outer rib that grab the frame rails, or printed push
  rivets through the existing rail holes.
- **Side panels.** Only the flat panel exists; sides need height and corner joints.
- **Printed fasteners.** The frame build still uses M3 bolts and nuts. Printed
  screws and nuts, or replacing the frame's half lap bolts with the same butterfly
  keys used here, would make the whole case bought-hardware free.
